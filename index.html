I built this because I kept getting stopped out at the exact low, and no chart in India would tell me why.

Every order-flow platform worth having is built for CME futures. Point one at NIFTY 50 and it declines to draw — correctly, because an index has no order book and no volume. So the most traded index in the world by contract count is also the one you are least able to see into, and the answer from the tooling market was that this is simply how it is.

It is not. Dhan exposes a 200-level depth feed on the near-month future. Nobody stores it, so EIMC stores it — once a second, from 09:00, whether the app is open or not — and shifts it onto the index by the basis. That one decision is most of why this product exists, and it is the reason the heatmap and the walls can appear on a chart that, strictly speaking, has no liquidity of its own.

The rest follows from not wanting to be lied to. The volume profile is checked row by row against a real TradingView screenshot. Bar replay is proven blind to the future by a test that corrupts every future candle and demands a byte-identical payload. The DQRS hit rates are measured on your symbol, stop-first, because a number I chose would be worthless. Where I could write a test instead of a claim, I wrote the test.

It is new, and I would rather you did not take my word for any of it. The free tier is the whole interface with no account and no expiry, and the Pro trial takes no card. Run it against demo data tonight, then point it at the market for three days. If it does not show you something your current chart could not, it has failed and it costs you nothing.
